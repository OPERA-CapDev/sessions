Welcome

Ocean forecasting is more than a model.

Ocean forecasts come from a connected system: observations, data assimilation, numerical models, forecast products and the services that support real-world decisions.

How do these parts work together? What happens when one of them is weak? And how does ocean information reach the people who need it? Today we explore the whole system, discover its applications, and find where each of us can contribute.

This interactive webinar builds on the OPERA MOOC Fundamentals of Ocean Forecasting and prepares you for the hands-on workshops.

By the end of today, you will be able to

  1. Understand the complete forecasting systemExplain how its components connect, from the first observation to the final user.
  2. Recognise why every component mattersSpot the weak points in the chain and what they cause downstream.
  3. Connect forecasts to real decisionsWho uses ocean information, why they need it, and how it supports their activities.
  4. Explore African applicationsExisting ocean forecasting initiatives and their relevance to regional needs.
  5. Discover Copernicus MarineA working service that brings the whole system together, and our focus on Days 2 and 3.

What users need

  • Accurate, reliable information
  • Timely: ready before the decision is made
  • Accessible and easy to interpret (FAIR data)
  • An honest statement of uncertainty: skill, confidence, limits

Even an accurate forecast has little value if it arrives too late or cannot be understood.

Case studies

Benguela upwelling fisheries · tropical cyclone warnings in the Southwest Indian Ocean · coastal flooding along the Gulf of Guinea · coral reefs in the Western Indian Ocean · the MV Wakashio oil spill, Mauritius, August 2020.

A working example of the chain

The Copernicus Marine Service is the European Union's ocean monitoring and forecasting service, implemented by Mercator Ocean International. It runs every link of the chain we drew today, every day, and its products are free and open to everyone, including for African waters.

The chain, inside Copernicus Marine

Three kinds of product

ObservationsSatellite and in situ: sea surface temperature, sea level, ocean colour, sea ice, waves, Argo floats, moorings.
Analyses and forecastsNear real time: today's ocean and the next 10 days, updated daily (global physics at 1/12°).
ReanalysesMulti-year, consistent reconstructions of the past ocean (global since 1993) for climate and statistics.

Products are organised around the blue (physics), white (sea ice) and green (biogeochemistry) ocean. Coverage: the global ocean plus European regional seas. There is no dedicated West African regional product, so we work with the global products.
