1

Mauritania / Senegal

Canary upwelling

Cold, rich water along one of the world’s great fishing coasts.

  • Trade winds pull deep, cold water to the surface: coastal upwelling, eddies and 48-hour inertial loops.
  • Day 2 — The thermocline off Cap Blanc and how sea level follows it.
  • Day 3 — Forecasts for fisheries in Nouakchott and Nouadhibou · a pirogue adrift · a marine-heatwave watch.

Places: Nouadhibou · Nouakchott · Dakar

2

Gulf of Guinea — east

Equator, monsoon and rivers

The equatorial ocean, monsoon winds and the outflow of great rivers.

  • Fast, straight coastal currents; at the equator the Earth’s rotation barely acts — no inertial loops.
  • Day 2 — Sea level and the thermocline across the equator (section at 6°E).
  • Day 3 — Forecasts for port and offshore operations at Port-Gentil · a person overboard off Cap Lopez · a heatwave watch.

Places: Bight of Biafra · Port-Gentil · Pointe-Noire

3

Agulhas

One of the fastest currents on Earth

A current of up to 2 m/s that sheds giant eddies into the Atlantic.

  • A western boundary current: deep eddies, wind against current and dangerous seas. Southern Hemisphere — the seasons are reversed.
  • Day 2 — Sea level, eddy energy and a section across the Agulhas retroflection.
  • Day 3 — Forecasts for shipping on the East London route · a seafarer overboard · heatwaves below the surface.

Places: Cape Point · Mossel Bay · East London
